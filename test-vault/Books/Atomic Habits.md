# Atomic Habits

A book about changing behavior through small, repeatable steps rather than big goals. My summary, in my own words.

## The four laws

1. **Make it obvious**: put the cue where you can't miss it (running shoes by the door).
2. **Make it attractive**: pair the habit with something you already like.
3. **Make it easy**: shrink the habit until starting takes less than two minutes.
4. **Make it satisfying**: give yourself an immediate reward, even a tick in a tracker.

To break a bad habit, invert each law: hide the cue, make it unattractive, add friction, make it unsatisfying.

## Ideas I'm using

- **Habit stacking**: attach a new habit to an existing one. After coffee, I write three lines in the journal (see [[2026-10-07]]).
- **Identity over outcomes**: "I'm someone who runs" holds up better than "I want to finish a half marathon".
- **Environment design** beats willpower ([[2026-10-15]]).

## Compared with Deep Work

[[Deep Work]] is about protecting long stretches of focus; this book is about the small automatic behaviors that make those stretches possible. They work well together: the deep work block itself became a habit once it had a fixed cue.
